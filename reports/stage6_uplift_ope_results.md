## 1. Learned vs true effects (T-learner, train window)

| action | naive r (confounded) | IPW r (learned) | true r | IPW d | true d | frozen assumption (r, d) |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| VERIFY | 0.280 | 0.466 | 0.232 | 0.017 | 0.030 | (0.30, 0.05) |
| DEPOSIT | 0.741 | 0.745 | 0.495 | 0.353 | 0.281 | (0.80, 0.40) |
| PREPAID | 0.838 | 0.857 | 0.300 | 0.583 | 0.612 | (0.55, 0.70) |

**The estimation lesson — read this twice**. BOTH the naive and the IPW T-learner sit far above the truth for DEPOSIT and VERIFY; propensity weighting fixes selection into treatment, not extrapolation. Each arm model is fit on a 2–5% sliver of orders and must then predict the whole population, and the ratio r = 1 − p̂_a/p̂_allow amplifies any under-prediction of p̂_allow (floor-clipped at 0.05). A merchant who 'validates' the 0.80 prior against logged data would measure 0.75 and call it confirmed — the truth is 0.50. Only ground truth reveals the gap, and in production there is none: that is exactly the case for DR-style corrections, larger exploration floors, and parametric effect priors. The one effect the data CAN see is the PREPAID drop rate (0.58 learned vs 0.61 true) — a 55–70% signal is loud enough to survive 2% coverage.

## 2. Policy comparison — festive test window, ground-truth outcomes

Baseline (always allow) portfolio loss: ₹-636,445 over 16,557 COD orders.

|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| P1 Constants (v1) | ₹516,182 | 74% | 14.5% | 42.5% | 42.9% | 0.0% |
| P2 Global-learned | ₹528,738 | 76% | 0.9% | 74.0% | 15.5% | 9.6% |
| P3 Uplift (heterogeneous) | ₹464,310 | 67% | 11.4% | 42.6% | 31.7% | 14.2% |
| P4 Oracle (true effects) | ₹698,133 | 100% | 19.6% | 34.1% | 46.2% | 0.0% |

Reading: P1's frozen constants assume deposit r = 0.80 against a truth of 0.50, yet still capture 74% of oracle value — a wrong-but-centered constant is not fatal. P2 (one learned (r, d) per action) is the best deployable policy at 76%: +₹12,557 over P1 for learning six numbers. P3 (per-order heterogeneous effects) TRAILS P1 at 67%: its order-level effect estimates are noisier than the constant's bias (§1), so mis-ranking effects order-by-order costs more than it saves. The oracle's headroom over P1 — ₹181,952, 26 points of value — is the combined price of assuming instead of estimating AND of estimating badly on 2–5% arm coverage. Uplift is not free; it needs data.

## 3. Off-policy evaluation — validation window

Reference: the legacy logging policy scores ₹-56.65 loss/order (savings vs always-allow: ₹33,061). Can offline estimators rank the candidates before deployment?

| policy | true loss (₹/order) | IPS | SNIPS | doubly robust | IPS bias | DR bias |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| P1 Constants (v1) | -77.20 | -68.37 | -62.90 | -61.03 | +11.4% | +20.9% |
| P2 Global-learned | -76.92 | -51.98 | -49.08 | -71.40 | +32.4% | +7.2% |
| P3 Uplift (heterogeneous) | -71.77 | -56.27 | -60.43 | -76.50 | +21.6% | -6.6% |
| P4 Oracle (true effects) | -84.36 | -70.65 | -68.97 | -76.49 | +16.2% | +9.3% |

No estimator is a substitute for overlap. Raw IPS ran +11–+32% optimistic on every policy (thin DEPOSIT/PREPAID coverage → rare matches → exploding weights). DR cut bias to a -7–+21% band — single digits on 3 of 4 policies, but 21 pts on P1 Constants (v1), whose action menu diverges most from what the logging policy actually did. The deployable pattern: keep exploration floors in the router, run DR (never raw IPS) before any policy swap, and distrust candidates whose actions diverge far from the logged ones.

## 5. Takeaways

1. **A wrong constant survived — barely**: P1's assumed r=0.80 (truth 0.50) still took 74% of oracle; the remaining ₹181,952 (26 pts) is what not knowing the effects costs. Global learning (P2) recovered the first ₹12,557 of that with six learned numbers.
2. **Heterogeneous uplift did NOT beat a good constant here**: P3 took only 67% of oracle — below P1 (74%) — because per-order effect estimates on 2–5% arm coverage are noisier than the bias they correct (§1). Uplift pays only with real coverage or stronger estimators (causal forests, DR-learners, monotone effect priors).
3. **Off-policy evaluation is a guardrail, not an oracle**: DR bias ranged -6.6% to +20.9% (worst on P1 Constants (v1)), raw IPS +11–+32% everywhere. Exploration floors are not optional — without them none of these estimators even exist.
4. **The production recipe**: exploration floors → logged propensities → learn global (r, d) first → heterogeneous uplift only once arm coverage supports it → DR-based offline audit → staged rollout with drift monitoring (§4: PR-AUC fell exactly when RTO spiked).
